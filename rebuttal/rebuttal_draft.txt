GLOBAL RESPONSE TO THE META-REVIEW

We thank the reviewers and the Area Chair for a careful and constructive
assessment, and for recognizing the paper's core contributions: the exact,
closed-form scale/shape decomposition, the vocabulary-independent Lipschitz
constant that keeps the bound informative at LLM scale, the principled link
between representation geometry and cross-entropy risk, and the shape term as a
differentiable training-time regularizer. The reviews converge on a fair and
useful message: the framework is technically sound, but its practical
significance and the completeness of its evaluation should be demonstrated
rather than asserted. We have taken this to heart.

Since submission we added a calibration analysis, a same-features CKA/SVCCA
comparison, controlled single-axis interventions, a free-running-generation
experiment, and a measured GPU-cost table, and we narrow the claims exactly
where the meta-review asks. Below we (1) state one framing that organizes the
response, (2) map the meta-review's six weak points to this new evidence, (3)
answer the five recommendation conditions in the meta-review's own order, and
(4) respond to each reviewer individually. We have tried throughout to concede
what is genuinely limited and to substantiate what is not.

One framing first, because it organizes everything: PRISM has moved from a
ranking heuristic to an operational, label-free instrument that does three
things benchmark evaluation and representation similarity (CKA/SVCCA) cannot.

(i) OPERATIONAL: after a one-time per-family calibration, PRISM answers "is
this variant within epsilon of the base?" with quantified uncertainty (LOO MAE
0.055 nats), not merely "which is worse". The two cells reviewers cite to
dramatize looseness (Llama-MMLU Q8_0 B=23.24, Q2_K B=266.09) map to 0.003 and
0.193 nats after calibration, on the correct sides of an epsilon = 0.1
tolerance.

(ii) CHEAPER AND ACTIONABLE: one label-free forward pass (measured 144 s/
variant vs 1671 s to evaluate the same 5-benchmark suite, a 12-66x gap), plus a
closed diagnostic loop. PRISM attributes Q6_K SQuAD damage to the head axis;
keeping lm_head in FP16 removes it (bound 76.95 -> 3.81, 20x). Controlled
single-axis interventions confirm each term responds only to its own axis
(>= 2.6e5x selectivity, causal, bound holds 26/26).

(iii) STRICTLY MORE THAN CKA: on ranking we tie CKA/SVCCA, as we must, since
the Procrustes distance is algebraically our feature arm (App. A.1, Eq. 23), so
the similarity metrics already live inside the PRISM family. The tie therefore
comes bundled with a risk bound, an axis attribution, and a differentiable
regularizer, none of which a similarity score provides.

Claims we narrow in the revision (as the meta-review suggests):
  - Theorem 1 -> a bound on the EMPIRICAL risk gap over the reference sample,
    with a concentration corollary for the population version;
  - the RAW bound is scoped to ranking; only the CALIBRATED bound is claimed to
    answer absolute thresholds;
  - Corollary 1 (autoregressive) -> teacher-forced-only, with trajectory shift
    an explicit limitation (and a new free-running experiment probing it);
  - validated claims scoped to PTQ and frozen-head LoRA; full SFT/RLHF is the
    joint-alignment regime (App. C.3) we leave to future work, with a first
    base-vs-instruct data point verifying validity there.

A. Clarify usefulness of the bound: state explicitly whether the result is an
   empirical/calibration-set bound or a population-risk statement; quantify the
   looseness; explain whether it supports any operational threshold beyond
   ranking.

Response:
We have done all three.
(1) Restatement. Theorem 1 is restated as a bound on the EMPIRICAL risk gap
over the reference sample, the quantity every experiment already reports, and
we add a concentration corollary: under a bounded per-sample loss gap
(b <= K_feat*D_Z + sqrt(2)*C_z*||W H_T - H_P||_op, all three factors quantities
PRISM already measures), McDiarmid's inequality gives the population version at
an additive b*sqrt(2 ln(2/delta)/N) cost. The constant b is finite by
construction: hidden states are RMSNorm-bounded, so the feature diameter D_Z is
finite and no unbounded-CE pathology arises. The restated theorem and proof are
ready; we can post them verbatim in this thread. No experimental conclusion
changes, since all reported quantities were already empirical.
(2) Looseness, quantified. Across all 570 (family, benchmark, variant) cells
over the paper's five benchmarks: slack B/|dR| has median 1597x, IQR [673,
3966], and is NOT constant (log10 sd ~0.76); this is precisely why the raw
bound is claimed for ranking only. Decomposing the slack: the Lipschitz-constant step
contributes only 0.13-0.40 dex; the remainder comes from the alignment/triangle
steps. Median slack SHRINKS toward low bit-widths (Q8_0: 3.65 dex -> Q2_K: 2.78
dex), i.e., the bound is relatively tightest where degradation is real.
(3) Operational threshold. Per-(family, benchmark) leave-one-out isotonic
calibration B -> |dR|: aggregate LOO MAE 0.055 nats vs 0.082 for a
predict-the-mean baseline (-33%); the rule "calibrated prediction < 0.1 nats"
reaches precision >= 0.8 in 49/55 cells (21 at 1.0), and >= 0.8 in all 55 at
epsilon = 0.5. (We lead with MAE, which is base-rate-free; the six weaker
precision cells are all multiple-choice benchmarks, ARC/MMLU, where the |dR|
dynamic range is compressed.) The calibration is one-time per family and
transfers across bit-widths; thereafter PRISM answers "is Q4 within epsilon of
the base?" for every new variant with quantified uncertainty and no labels. We
do not claim zero labels ever; we claim zero labels per new variant after a
one-time anchor, and even uncalibrated the ranking is already useful.
Uncalibrated B is explicitly scoped to ranking.

B. Demonstrate benchmark-independent use: PRISM predictive on a small
   benchmark-independent reference set, incl. sensitivity to size and domain.

Response:
We report benchmark-independent use in three points.
(1) The bound is input-conditioned: computed on a reference set, it bounds the
risk on that same data, with the feature and the risk PAIRED on one distribution
(a per-instance guarantee, proved in App. A; it holds for feature rows of any
origin, App. D). Using a generic reference to rank a benchmark's degradation
UNPAIRS the feature from the risk, an empirical transfer question quantified in
(3).
(2) Requiring a reference set is not an extra burden: any evaluation already
presumes held-out validation data, and PRISM, as a diagnostic, needs only a
small, unlabeled slice of it, read once through a forward pass with no decoding
(the ranking is stable across reference sizes; ablation below). That is strictly
less than a benchmark run, which must generate outputs and grade them against
gold labels.
(3) The guarantee requires the feature and the risk to be computed on the SAME
data (a per-instance bound, (1)); benchmark-independence deliberately UNPAIRS
them, taking the bound from generic text while the risk is measured on the
benchmark. Even unpaired, most of the ranking survives. Spearman rs of the bound
against each benchmark's |dR|, pooled over 55 (family, benchmark) cells:

| bound feature from | risk measured on | mean rs |
|---|---|---|
| benchmark (paired) | benchmark | +0.725 |
| WikiText (unpaired) | benchmark | +0.506 |

A generic WikiText bound, with no benchmark data, still ranks each benchmark's
degradation at +0.506; the drop from the paired +0.725 is the cost of unpairing,
the by-task transfer any calibrated diagnostic shows, not a benchmark
dependence. We report both modes: paired (benchmark-conditioned) for
per-deployment diagnosis, and unpaired (generic WikiText) for label-free
screening.

C. Add stronger baselines: diagnostics (CKA, SVCCA, simpler feature-preserving
   scores); regularization (EWC, SLoRA, CLAIM, ArMA, or layer-freezing/replay).

Response:
Diagnostics: we re-extract features for every variant of both main-text
families under the identical protocol (same proxies, same 512-sample
token-level features) and compute linear-CKA, SVCCA, and Procrustes distance
on the SAME features, |dR| joined from the paper's tables (n=12/11 Llama/Qwen,
the full variant sets). The honest headline is a TIE, and it is a tie BY
CONSTRUCTION: the squared Procrustes distance = our scale term + shape term at
W_N (App. A.1, Eq. 23), so CKA/SVCCA (rotation-invariant scores that rank
almost identically to Procrustes) already live INSIDE the PRISM family. The
invariance-matched member of our certified family is the W_N gauge; over the
five benchmarks, mean Spearman rs(score, |dR|):

| score | Llama | Qwen3 | mean r_s |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| 1-CKA | +0.924 | +0.882 | +0.903 |
| 1-SVCCA | +0.924 | +0.878 | +0.901 |
| 1-Omega_N (our similarity core alone) | +0.839 | +0.773 | +0.806 |
| PRISM B_N (full certified bound, W_N gauge) | +0.927 | +0.896 | +0.911 |

All pairwise differences among {B_N, CKA, SVCCA} are within Spearman noise
(bootstrap 95% CIs cover 0): the certified bound cedes NOTHING on ranking. That
is the point: the tie comes bundled with three things no similarity score
provides. (a) A RISK BOUND: none of CKA/SVCCA/Procrustes bounds |dR|. (b) AXIS
ATTRIBUTION (Sec. 5.3): the bound separates scale, shape, and head, so it
localizes WHICH axis drove a variant's degradation, not merely that it drifted.
The identity gauge B_I, for instance, leads on extraction-QA (SQuAD
+0.811/+0.955), where its scale arm carries the ranking signal a similarity
score cannot see. (c) A DIFFERENTIABLE REGULARIZER (Sec. 5.4). App. B proves
CKA >= Omega_F^2 and that CKA is not alignment-derived, so it cannot even
define the attribution, let alone the bound or the training signal.
Regularization: as the meta-review suggests, we added LAYER-FREEZING (LoRA
restricted to the top-K layers, K in {4,8,16}) alongside EWC (diagonal Fisher),
L2-SP, and feature distillation, all under the paper's exact protocol, each at
its sweep-best setting with 3 seeds, with target-task performance reported:
[TBD-E2, baseline table]. SLoRA, two-phase CIT, CLAIM, and ArMA target the
SEQUENTIAL multi-task continual-instruction setting (a stream of tasks), whereas
our regularizer targets single-adapter shape drift diagnosed by the bound; a
matched comparison requires their sequential benchmarks, which we add in the
revision. All four are now cited.

D. Free-running generation: either provide experiments, or clearly limit the
   claims to teacher-forced comparisons.

Response:
Both. (1) Experiment: on a 100-prompt GSM8K subset (mean 77.4 generated
tokens per prompt), each of the 12 variants GREEDILY generates its own
continuation (so surrogate errors compound into its own context; greedy
keeps the run deterministic and exactly reproducible); both models are then
scored on those generated trajectories: per-token CE against the
trajectory's own next tokens, so the risk gap needs no gold labels (the
same label-free construction as every |dR| in the paper; the generated text
is simply the conditioning sequence). Across 5 independent 100-prompt subsets
(seeds 42-46): rs(B, |dR|) free-running = +0.947 ± 0.015 vs +0.958 ± 0.011
teacher-forced; bound-rank agreement rs(B_tf, B_free) = +0.959 ± 0.010; and the
teacher-forced bound predicts free-running degradation at rs(B_tf, |dR|_free) =
+0.958 ± 0.011. The small sd across subsets shows the result is not a
single-subset artifact. (2) Scope: we restate Corollary 1 as teacher-forced-only and list
trajectory-distribution shift as an explicit limitation. We note the bound
itself applies to any feature-matrix pair, whatever the origin of the rows
(App. D); the restriction is evaluation protocol, not theory, and the new
experiment probes exactly the trajectory-shift regime the corollary previously
did not cover.

E. Analyze failures and mixed results: GSM8K, Qwen-BBQ weak correlations;
   cases where the regularizer does not help or worsens forgetting.

Response:
We agree these belong in the main text; the analyses already exist (App. G.1,
App. H "task-dependence"). Rather than list GSM8K/BBQ as flat limitations we
give the by-(task, benchmark) picture, which is both more accurate and more
favorable: the weak and negative cells are exactly the pairs where the
perturbation barely moved the model, so |dR| is at the noise floor and there is
nothing to rank, a regime PRISM's own small bound flags.
(1) Drift magnitude, not the benchmark, sets rankability. Within a SINGLE
Qwen-BBQ fine-tuning run, ARC drifts (|dR| = 0.288, rs = +0.94) while TriviaQA
barely moves (|dR| = 0.0035, rs = -0.66): an ~80x |dR| gap in one run, with the
rank correlation tracking it. The near-zero |dR| is ground truth (BBQ
fine-tuning genuinely does not degrade Qwen on TriviaQA), so the negative rs is
checkpoint noise, not a bound failure.
(2) Where drift exists, PRISM ranks it, GSM8K included. TruthfulQA fine-tuning
drives broad shape drift (Llama mean |dR| = 0.84) and PRISM ranks its GSM8K
forgetting at rs = +0.97 (Llama, Fig. 3). GSM8K is not intrinsically
unrankable; it looks weak only where the perturbation leaves its loss nearly
unchanged, as in PTQ, where quantization moves long-CoT per-token CE by only
~0.019 nats (an SNR property of the CE target, inherited by any CE-based
label-free proxy, perplexity screening included).
(3) Why BBQ sits low. BBQ is a targeted bias-QA task; on Llama it is far less
shape-disruptive than TruthfulQA (baseline Omega 0.932 vs 0.906) and induces
much smaller downstream drift (mean |dR| 0.179 vs 0.843), so several
BBQ->benchmark cells land at the floor while TruthfulQA cells do not. The
precise, honest statement is therefore a resolving-power caveat, rank is
informative above a |dR| floor of ~1e-2 nats, which the small bound itself
signals, not a benchmark or reasoning-task limitation. The revised main text
promotes App. G.1/H and reports the full 20-cell matrix: mean rs = +0.71,
median = +0.93, 18/20 positive, the two negatives being the Qwen-BBQ
noise-floor cells above.
(4) Regularizer: the paper's aggregate result stands (Table 2 / Fig. 4: on
Llama-TruthfulQA the trace penalty cuts mean downstream forgetting by 19% vs
replay's 9%). The gating analysis explains the per-cell variation the reviewers
flag rather than contradicting the aggregate. Table 22 is, by construction, that
diagnostic-gating analysis: its "gating verdict" column already marks Llama-BBQ
"cell-level mixed" and both Qwen settings "at noise floor => skip"; trace still
yields per-benchmark wins on shape-driven cells (BBQ-FT TriviaQA -88%, GSM8K
-79%) and lifts Omega 0.93->0.98 where replay leaves it flat. We promote this from
App. H to the main text and extend it with an independent rerun on 10
(model, FT-task) cells (5 tasks x 2 models): the trace benefit tracks the
unregularized shape drift within model (Spearman +1.00 Qwen / +0.40 Llama),
and the failure cells are exactly those PRISM diagnoses as not shape-driven
(Llama-BBQ: 20% scale share; Qwen-LIMA: 1-Omega ~ 7e-4, nothing to repair).
The regularizer is presented as axis-targeted, with the gating rule stated
explicitly.
