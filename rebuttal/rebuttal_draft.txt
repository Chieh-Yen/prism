Meta Review (AC)
AC LN7U (17 Jul 2026, modified 24 Jul 2026), Preliminary: Borderline Reject, "What could change the recommendation?"

···· ORIGINAL META-REVIEW — verbatim from OpenReview (for 一一對應檢查; internal, DO NOT POST) ····
[Summary / Metareview]
The paper proposes PRISM, a framework for diagnosing degradation in variants of LLM
post-training, including quantized and LoRA-adapted models. The main technical claim is that
the cross-entropy risk gap between a target-base model and a proxy-variant model can be
upper-bounded by a closed-form geometric quantity decomposed into three interpretable
components: scale mismatch, shape mismatch, and head divergence. The authors argue that
these components correspond to different failure modes and that the differentiable shape
component can thus serve as a regularizer against catastrophic forgetting.

[Strong Points]
- Interesting and timely problem: All reviewers recognize that diagnosing post-trained LLM
  variant degradation is an important problem. PRISM attempts to go beyond measuring
  degradation and decomposes drift into interpretable components.
- Conceptually appealing decomposition: Reviewers generally find the decomposition into
  scale, shape, and head terms intuitive and potentially useful. pCi8 emphasizes that the
  scale/shape split is mathematically clean and exact, and G3T9 and 8VrD note that this
  decomposition is more informative than a single representation similarity score such as
  CKA or SVCCA.
- Connection between geometry and risk: Several reviewers appreciate the connection between
  representation geometry and cross-entropy risk. G3T9 identifies this as a key strength,
  and 8VrD highlights the hybrid risk construction that separates backbone drift from head
  drift.
- Promise as an empirical ranking tool: The reported rank correlations for PTQ and LoRA
  forgetting suggest that PRISM may be useful for ranking variants when direct evaluation is
  expensive. pCi8 and G3T9 both note the low computational cost and the potential practical
  value of using a small number of forward passes rather than a full benchmark suite.
- Shape regularization: The use of the differentiable shape term as a training-time
  regularizer is viewed positively in principle. pCi8, eQL6, and 8VrD all mention this as an
  attractive aspect of the paper, though they differ on how convincing the evidence is.

[Overall recommendation]
My preliminary recommendation is hovering around Borderline Reject. A strong and convincing
rebuttal with additional experiments and careful narrowing of the claims could move the
paper closer to borderline accept, but based on the current reviews, the weaknesses outweigh
the strengths.
(The AC's six Weak Points appear verbatim in the mapping below, each tagged [原文]; the five
"What could change the recommendation?" items appear verbatim as the A-E headings.)

GLOBAL RESPONSE TO THE META-REVIEW

We thank the reviewers and the Area Chair for a careful and constructive
assessment, and for recognizing the paper's core contributions: the exact,
closed-form scale/shape decomposition, the vocabulary-independent Lipschitz
constant that keeps the bound informative at LLM scale, the principled link
between representation geometry and cross-entropy risk, and the shape term as a
differentiable training-time regularizer. The reviews converge on a fair and
useful message: the framework is technically sound, but its practical
significance and the completeness of its evaluation should be demonstrated
rather than asserted. We have taken this to heart.

The meta-review asks that its five conditions be met with concrete evidence
rather than only clarification. We ran the experiments; each condition below is
answered with a measurement, and where a request exceeds what the theory
licenses (the unpaired half of B) we narrow the claim explicitly, as the
meta-review invites:

| condition | new evidence | headline |
|---|---|---|
| A. usefulness of the bound | isotonic calibration + slack decomposition | LOO MAE 0.055 nats; the two cells cited for looseness calibrate to 0.003 / 0.193 nats, each on the correct side of a 0.1-nat tolerance |
| B. benchmark-independent use | explicit scope narrowing + reference-set size ablation | the bound is input-conditioned by design: it needs only a small unlabeled slice of the diagnosed task's own validation inputs (no labels, no decoding); ranking size-invariant from 8 to 128 sequences; regularizer reference size-insensitive, mildly domain-sensitive (downstream 0.72-0.77) |
| C. stronger baselines | CKA/SVCCA on identical features; EWC / L2-SP / layer-freezing | bound ties CKA/SVCCA (CIs cover 0) and does strictly more; shape penalty ahead of EWC, L2-SP, layer-freezing, and replay at matched target-task loss (TruthfulQA so far) |
| D. free-running generation | greedy self-generation, 5 subsets | rs +0.947±0.015 free vs +0.958±0.011 teacher-forced; the teacher-forced bound predicts free-running degradation at +0.958±0.011 |
| E. failures and mixed results | full 20-cell matrix + GSM8K SNR analysis + an honestly reported failed mitigation | mean rs +0.71 / median +0.93, 18/20 positive; both negative cells sit at the noise floor |

Claims we narrow in the revision (as the meta-review suggests), conceding what
is genuinely limited and substantiating what is not:
  - Theorem 1 -> a bound on the EMPIRICAL risk gap over the reference sample,
    with a concentration corollary for the population version;
  - the RAW bound is scoped to ranking; only the CALIBRATED bound is claimed to
    answer absolute thresholds;
  - the reference set is the diagnosed task's own unlabeled inputs; predicting
    one distribution's degradation from another distribution's features
    (unpaired use) is outside the derivation and not claimed;
  - Corollary 1 (autoregressive) -> teacher-forced-only, with trajectory shift
    an explicit limitation (and a new free-running experiment probing it);
  - validated claims scoped to PTQ and frozen-head LoRA; full SFT/RLHF is the
    joint-alignment regime (App. C.3) we leave to future work, with a first
    base-vs-instruct data point verifying validity there.

What remains after this narrowing is the significance case: PRISM is an
operational, label-free instrument that does three things benchmark evaluation
and representation similarity (CKA/SVCCA) cannot.

(i) OPERATIONAL: after a one-time per-family calibration, PRISM answers "is
this variant within epsilon of the base?" with quantified uncertainty (LOO MAE
0.055 nats), not merely "which is worse". The two cells reviewers cite to
dramatize looseness (Llama-MMLU Q8_0 B=23.24, Q2_K B=266.09) map to 0.003 and
0.193 nats after calibration, on the correct sides of an epsilon = 0.1
tolerance.

(ii) CHEAPER AND ACTIONABLE: one label-free teacher-forced pass, no decoding and no
grading. On GSM8K, the generation-heaviest benchmark, a single greedy decode of the
same prompts costs a measured 62.6x that pass (501x with self-consistency). The
diagnosis also closes into an act-and-verify loop: PRISM attributes Q6_K SQuAD
damage to the head axis; keeping lm_head in FP16 removes it (bound
76.95 -> 3.81, 20x), and controlled single-axis interventions confirm each term
responds only to its own axis (>= 2.6e5x selectivity, causal, bound holds 26/26).

(iii) STRICTLY MORE THAN CKA: on ranking we tie CKA/SVCCA, as we must, since
the Procrustes distance is algebraically our feature arm (App. A.1, Eq. 23), so
the similarity metrics already live inside the PRISM family. The tie therefore
comes bundled with a risk bound, an axis attribution, and a differentiable
regularizer, none of which a similarity score provides.

A [原文]: Clarify usefulness of the bound: The authors should explicitly state whether the
   result should be understood as an empirical/calibration-set bound or a population-risk
   statement. They should also quantify the looseness of the bound and explain whether it
   can support any operational threshold beyond ranking.

Response:
We have done all three.
(1) Restatement. Theorem 1 is restated as a bound on the EMPIRICAL risk gap
over the reference sample, the quantity every experiment already reports, and
we add a concentration corollary: under a bounded per-sample loss gap
(b <= K_feat*D_Z + sqrt(2)*C_z*||W H_T - H_P||_op, all three factors quantities
PRISM already measures), McDiarmid's inequality gives the population version at
an additive b*sqrt(2 ln(2/delta)/N) cost. The constant b is finite by
construction: hidden states are RMSNorm-bounded, so the feature diameter D_Z is
finite and no unbounded-CE pathology arises. The restated theorem and proof are
ready; we can post them verbatim in this thread. No experimental conclusion
changes, since all reported quantities were already empirical.
(2) Looseness, quantified. Across all 570 (family, benchmark, variant) cells
over the paper's five benchmarks: slack B/|dR| has median 1597x, IQR [673,
3966], and is NOT constant (log10 sd ~0.76); this is precisely why the raw
bound is claimed for ranking only. Decomposing the slack: the Lipschitz-constant step
contributes only 0.13-0.40 dex; the remainder comes from the alignment/triangle
steps. Median slack SHRINKS toward low bit-widths (Q8_0: 3.65 dex -> Q2_K: 2.78
dex), i.e., the bound is relatively tightest where degradation is real.
(3) Operational threshold. Per-(family, benchmark) leave-one-out isotonic
calibration B -> |dR|: aggregate LOO MAE 0.055 nats vs 0.082 for a
predict-the-mean baseline (-33%); the rule "calibrated prediction < 0.1 nats"
reaches precision >= 0.8 in 49/55 cells (21 at 1.0), and >= 0.8 in all 55 at
epsilon = 0.5. (We lead with MAE, which is base-rate-free; the six weaker
precision cells are all multiple-choice benchmarks, ARC/MMLU, where the |dR|
dynamic range is compressed.) The calibration is one-time per family and
transfers across bit-widths; thereafter PRISM answers "is Q4 within epsilon of
the base?" for every new variant with quantified uncertainty and no labels. We
do not claim zero labels ever; we claim zero labels per new variant after a
one-time anchor, and even uncalibrated the ranking is already useful.
Uncalibrated B is explicitly scoped to ranking.

B [原文]: Demonstrate benchmark-independent use: The authors should show that PRISM remains
   predictive when computed on a small, benchmark-independent reference set, including
   sensitivity to reference-set size and domain.

Response:
PRISM does not need, and does not claim, a benchmark-independent reference: the
bound is input-conditioned by construction, so diagnosing a task means reading
a small UNLABELED slice of that task's own validation inputs, data any
evaluation already has. We therefore answer this condition in two parts, as the
meta-review invites: the out-of-scope half (unpaired use) with an explicit
narrowing of the claim, and the in-scope half (how small and stable the
reference can be) with a measurement.
(1) Scope, stated precisely. The bound is input-conditioned: computed on a
reference set, it bounds the risk gap on that same data, with the feature and
the risk PAIRED on one distribution (a per-instance guarantee, proved in
App. A; it holds for feature rows of any origin, App. D). Computing the bound
on generic text to predict a different benchmark's degradation would UNPAIR
feature from risk, a distribution-transfer question the derivation does not
cover, so we do not claim it; the revision states this scope in the
Limitations.
(2) The practical requirement behind the condition is minimal. The reference is
not benchmark labels or a curated calibration corpus; it is a handful of the
diagnosed task's own validation inputs. That single reference pass is strictly
less work than a benchmark run: PRISM reads the slice once with no decoding,
whereas a benchmark run must generate outputs and grade them against gold
labels.
(3) The in-scope measurement: sensitivity to reference-set size.
Two takeaways. (i) The ranking is
SIZE-INVARIANT: rs against each benchmark's |dR| is identical (to four decimals)
across sizes {8, 16, 32, 64, 128} and 3 draws per size, so even 8 sequences
reproduce the full ranking and 32 is not a lucky draw. (ii) The across-draw
NOISE IS SMALL AND SHRINKS WITH SIZE: the mean coefficient of variation falls
from 3.7% (n=8) to 1.2% (n=128). The ranking holds even where adjacent bounds
are close because variants sharing a reference set shift together (common-mode),
so the per-draw order is preserved in all 15 settings. Bound_I, mean +- sd over
3 draws (Llama):

C [原文]: Add stronger baselines: For diagnostics, direct comparisons to CKA, SVCCA, and
   simpler feature-preserving scores are needed. For regularization, stronger
   continual-learning baselines such as EWC, SLoRA, CLAIM, ArMA, or layer-freezing/replay
   variants would substantially improve the evaluation.

Response:
Both baseline families now exist under the paper's exact protocol, and the
headline is deliberately honest: on ranking the certified bound TIES CKA/SVCCA
(a tie by construction, since they live inside the PRISM family) while adding
what a similarity score cannot, and on regularization the shape penalty leads
EWC, L2-SP, layer-freezing, and replay at matched target-task loss on
TruthfulQA so far.
Diagnostics: we re-extract features for every variant of both main-text
families under the identical protocol (same proxies, same 512-sample
token-level features) and compute linear-CKA, SVCCA, and Procrustes distance
on the SAME features, |dR| joined from the paper's tables (n=12/11 Llama/Qwen,
the full variant sets). The honest headline is a TIE, and it is a tie BY
CONSTRUCTION: the squared Procrustes distance = our scale term + shape term at
W_N (App. A.1, Eq. 23), so CKA/SVCCA (rotation-invariant scores that rank
almost identically to Procrustes) already live INSIDE the PRISM family. The
invariance-matched member of our certified family is the W_N gauge; over the
five benchmarks, mean Spearman rs(score, |dR|):

| score | Llama | Qwen3 | mean r_s |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| 1-CKA | +0.924 | +0.882 | +0.903 |
| 1-SVCCA | +0.924 | +0.878 | +0.901 |
| 1-Omega_N (our similarity core alone) | +0.839 | +0.773 | +0.806 |
| PRISM B_N (full certified bound, W_N gauge) | +0.927 | +0.896 | +0.911 |

All pairwise differences among {B_N, CKA, SVCCA} are within Spearman noise
(bootstrap 95% CIs cover 0): the certified bound cedes NOTHING on ranking. That
is the point: the tie comes bundled with three things no similarity score
provides. (a) A RISK BOUND: none of CKA/SVCCA/Procrustes bounds |dR|. (b) AXIS
ATTRIBUTION (Sec. 5.3): the bound separates scale, shape, and head, so it
localizes WHICH axis drove a variant's degradation, not merely that it drifted.
The identity gauge B_I, for instance, leads on extraction-QA (SQuAD
+0.811/+0.955), where its scale arm carries the ranking signal a similarity
score cannot see. (c) A DIFFERENTIABLE REGULARIZER (Sec. 5.4). App. B proves
CKA >= Omega_F^2 and that CKA is not alignment-derived, so it cannot even
define the attribution, let alone the bound or the training signal.
Regularization: the shape regularizer is a demonstration that the framework's diagnosed
axis is actionable, so its matched baselines are general forgetting methods in our
single-task setting. As the meta-review suggests, we add layer-freezing (LoRA on the
top-K layers, K in {4,8,16}) alongside EWC (diagonal Fisher) and L2-SP, all under the
paper's exact protocol, each reported at the config whose target-task loss matches the
shape run's (equal plasticity, so a baseline is not rewarded for under-training). On
TruthfulQA so far (seed 42; downstream = mean |dR| over the five held-out benchmarks):
the shape penalty (0.687) beats no-reg (0.843), replay (0.764), EWC (0.750),
L2-SP (0.747), and layer-freezing (which lowers |dR| only by under-training the FT
task, target loss 0.92-1.02 vs 0.87); seeds 43/44 are completing. SLoRA, two-phase CIT, CLAIM, and ArMA are
specialized for the sequential continual-instruction setting (a task stream), a
different problem than our single fine-tuning; we cite all four and leave a matched
comparison there to future work.

D [原文]: Free-running generation: The authors should either provide free-running
   generation experiments or clearly limit the claims to teacher-forced comparisons.

Response:
Both. (1) Experiment: on a 100-prompt GSM8K subset (mean 77.4 generated
tokens per prompt), each of the 12 variants GREEDILY generates its own
continuation (so surrogate errors compound into its own context; greedy
keeps the run deterministic and exactly reproducible); both models are then
scored on those generated trajectories: per-token CE against the
trajectory's own next tokens, so the risk gap needs no gold labels (the
same label-free construction as every |dR| in the paper; the generated text
is simply the conditioning sequence). Across 5 independent 100-prompt subsets
(seeds 42-46): rs(B, |dR|) free-running = +0.947 ± 0.015 vs +0.958 ± 0.011
teacher-forced; bound-rank agreement rs(B_tf, B_free) = +0.959 ± 0.010; and the
teacher-forced bound predicts free-running degradation at rs(B_tf, |dR|_free) =
+0.958 ± 0.011. The small sd across subsets shows the result is not a
single-subset artifact. (2) Scope: we restate Corollary 1 as teacher-forced-only and list
trajectory-distribution shift as an explicit limitation. We note the bound
itself applies to any feature-matrix pair, whatever the origin of the rows
(App. D); the restriction is evaluation protocol, not theory, and the new
experiment probes exactly the trajectory-shift regime the corollary previously
did not cover.

E [原文]: Analyze failures and mixed results: The authors should discuss weaker
   correlations, such as those noted for GSM8K or Qwen-BBQ, and cases where the regularizer
   does not help or worsens forgetting.

Response:
We agree these belong in the main text, and the promoted analysis is
quantitative, not a caveat list: over all 20 (model, FT-task, benchmark) cells
the rank correlation is mean rs +0.71 / median +0.93 with 18/20 positive, and
every weak or negative cell is a pair where the perturbation barely moved the
model, so |dR| sits at the noise floor and there is nothing to rank, a regime
PRISM's own small bound flags (App. G.1 / App. H "task-dependence", promoted).
(1) Drift magnitude, not the benchmark, sets rankability. Within a SINGLE
Qwen-BBQ fine-tuning run, ARC drifts (|dR| = 0.288, rs = +0.94) while TriviaQA
barely moves (|dR| = 0.0035, rs = -0.66): an ~80x |dR| gap in one run, with the
rank correlation tracking it. The near-zero |dR| is ground truth (BBQ
fine-tuning genuinely does not degrade Qwen on TriviaQA), so the negative rs is
checkpoint noise, not a bound failure.
(2) Where drift exists, PRISM ranks it, GSM8K included. TruthfulQA fine-tuning

[Summary]
PRISM is a diagnostic tool for post-training LLM variants — quantized, LoRA fine-tuned, or
distilled. The core problem it solves is pretty simple: when you quantize a model, you want
to know how much you broke it and where, without running a full benchmark suite. Existing
similarity metrics like CKA and SVCCA can tell you that a variant has drifted from the base,
but not why or which component is responsible. PRISM derives a closed-form upper bound on
the cross-entropy risk gap between a base model and its variant, decomposed into three
independently measurable axes: scale mismatch (activation magnitude collapse), shape
mismatch (distortion of the relative geometry between token representations), and head
divergence (the prediction head itself drifting). The key technical insight is that the
linear lm_head plus near-isometric backbone structure of modern LLMs lets you express this
bound tightly — avoiding the naive Lipschitz constant that would blow up with vocabulary
size — and the Procrustes residual gives you an exact scale/shape decomposition for free.
Empirically it ranks PTQ variants with Spearman rs = 0.820 and LoRA forgetting checkpoints
at rs = 0.831 across two model families and five benchmarks, using only a single forward
pass per model with no labels. The shape term is also differentiable, so it doubles as a
regularizer during LoRA fine-tuning that outperforms experience replay at mitigating
catastrophic forgetting.

[Strengths]
- Exact decomposition. The scale/shape split is an exact identity, not an approximation.
  The math is clean and the derivation is self-contained.
- Tighter Lipschitz constant. The simplex polarization trick is a real contribution — the
  naive spectral bound scales with vocabulary size and becomes useless at V~10⁵, this one
  doesn't.
- Three axes are actually distinct. Empirically they do capture different failure modes
  rather than just being correlated proxies of the same thing.
- Low compute cost. One forward pass per model, no labels needed. Useful in practice when
  you're choosing between a dozen quantization variants and don't want to run full
  benchmarks on all of them.
- Shape regularizer is a natural byproduct. You get a differentiable training objective for
  free from the bound, and it actually outperforms replay in the experiments.

2) W2 [原文]: In the theoretical analysis, the motivation for considering orthogonal

We restrict alignment to a rotation and a global scale, not an arbitrary linear map,
because an arbitrary map could be folded into the linear head and fit away the very
degradation we are trying to measure, whereas rotation-and-scale leaves a meaningful
residual and admits an exact scale/shape split. Three reasons make this precise (Sec. 3).

yielding the EXACT scale/shape split (Prop. 1); no larger group admits this.

models align well under maps that are in practice near-orthogonal, which we
also observe; App. B further shows the resulting Omega_N is the
alignment-grounded similarity (unlike CKA, which is not derived from any
alignment optimization).
