Meta Review (AC)
AC LN7U (17 Jul 2026, modified 24 Jul 2026), Preliminary: Borderline Reject, "What could change the recommendation?"

···· ORIGINAL META-REVIEW — verbatim from OpenReview (for 一一對應檢查; internal, DO NOT POST) ····
[Summary / Metareview]
The paper proposes PRISM, a framework for diagnosing degradation in variants of LLM
post-training, including quantized and LoRA-adapted models. The main technical claim is that
the cross-entropy risk gap between a target-base model and a proxy-variant model can be
upper-bounded by a closed-form geometric quantity decomposed into three interpretable
components: scale mismatch, shape mismatch, and head divergence. The authors argue that
these components correspond to different failure modes and that the differentiable shape
component can thus serve as a regularizer against catastrophic forgetting.

[Strong Points]
- Interesting and timely problem: All reviewers recognize that diagnosing post-trained LLM
  variant degradation is an important problem. PRISM attempts to go beyond measuring
  degradation and decomposes drift into interpretable components.
- Conceptually appealing decomposition: Reviewers generally find the decomposition into
  scale, shape, and head terms intuitive and potentially useful. pCi8 emphasizes that the
  scale/shape split is mathematically clean and exact, and G3T9 and 8VrD note that this
  decomposition is more informative than a single representation similarity score such as
  CKA or SVCCA.
- Connection between geometry and risk: Several reviewers appreciate the connection between
  representation geometry and cross-entropy risk. G3T9 identifies this as a key strength,
  and 8VrD highlights the hybrid risk construction that separates backbone drift from head
  drift.
- Promise as an empirical ranking tool: The reported rank correlations for PTQ and LoRA
  forgetting suggest that PRISM may be useful for ranking variants when direct evaluation is
  expensive. pCi8 and G3T9 both note the low computational cost and the potential practical
  value of using a small number of forward passes rather than a full benchmark suite.
- Shape regularization: The use of the differentiable shape term as a training-time
  regularizer is viewed positively in principle. pCi8, eQL6, and 8VrD all mention this as an
  attractive aspect of the paper, though they differ on how convincing the evidence is.

[Overall recommendation]
My preliminary recommendation is hovering around Borderline Reject. A strong and convincing
rebuttal with additional experiments and careful narrowing of the claims could move the
paper closer to borderline accept, but based on the current reviews, the weaknesses outweigh
the strengths.

GLOBAL RESPONSE TO THE META-REVIEW

We thank the reviewers and the Area Chair for a careful and constructive
assessment, and for recognizing the paper's core contributions: the exact,
closed-form scale/shape decomposition, the vocabulary-independent Lipschitz
constant that keeps the bound informative at LLM scale, the principled link
between representation geometry and cross-entropy risk, and the shape term as a
differentiable training-time regularizer. The reviews converge on a fair and
useful message: the framework is technically sound, but its practical
significance and the completeness of its evaluation should be demonstrated
rather than asserted. We have taken this to heart.

The meta-review asks that its five conditions be met with concrete evidence
rather than only clarification. We ran the experiments; each condition below is
answered with a measurement, and where a request exceeds what the theory
licenses (the unpaired half of B) we narrow the claim explicitly, as the
meta-review invites:

| condition | new evidence | headline |
|---|---|---|
| A. usefulness of the bound | isotonic calibration + slack decomposition | held-out error 0.055 nats; the two cells cited for looseness calibrate to 0.003 / 0.193 nats, each on the correct side of a 0.1-nat tolerance |
| B. benchmark-independent use | explicit scope narrowing + reference-set size ablation | the bound is input-conditioned by design: it needs only a small slice of the diagnosed task's own validation data, read once with no decoding and no grading; ranking size-invariant from 8 to 128 sequences; regularizer reference size-insensitive, mildly domain-sensitive (downstream 0.72-0.77) |
| C. stronger baselines | CKA/SVCCA on identical features; EWC / L2-SP / layer-freezing | bound ties CKA/SVCCA (CIs cover 0) and does strictly more; shape penalty ahead of EWC, L2-SP, layer-freezing, and replay at matched target-task loss (TruthfulQA so far) |
| D. free-running generation | greedy self-generation, 5 subsets | rs +0.947±0.015 free vs +0.958±0.011 teacher-forced; the teacher-forced bound predicts free-running degradation at +0.958±0.011 |
| E. failures and mixed results | full 20-cell matrix + GSM8K SNR analysis + an honestly reported failed mitigation | mean rs +0.71 / median +0.93, 18/20 positive; both negative cells sit at the noise floor |

Claims we narrow in the revision (as the meta-review suggests), conceding what
is genuinely limited and substantiating what is not:
  - Theorem 1 -> a bound on the EMPIRICAL risk gap over the reference sample,
    with a concentration corollary for the population version;
  - the RAW bound is scoped to ranking; only the CALIBRATED bound is claimed to
    answer absolute thresholds;
  - the reference set is a small slice of the diagnosed task's own validation
    data; predicting one distribution's degradation from another
    distribution's features (unpaired use) is outside the derivation and not
    claimed;
  - Corollary 1 (autoregressive) -> teacher-forced-only, with trajectory shift
    an explicit limitation (and a new free-running experiment probing it);
  - validated claims scoped to PTQ and frozen-head LoRA; full SFT/RLHF is the
    joint-alignment regime (App. C.3) we leave to future work, with a first
    base-vs-instruct data point verifying validity there.

What remains after this narrowing is the significance case: PRISM is an
operational, generation-free instrument that does three things benchmark
evaluation and representation similarity (CKA/SVCCA) cannot.

(i) OPERATIONAL: after a one-time per-family calibration, PRISM answers "is
this variant within epsilon of the base?" with quantified uncertainty (a typical
0.055 nats on a variant the calibration was not fitted on), not merely "which is
worse". The two cells reviewers cite to
dramatize looseness (Llama-MMLU Q8_0 B=23.24, Q2_K B=266.09) map to 0.003 and
0.193 nats after calibration, on the correct sides of an epsilon = 0.1
tolerance.

(ii) CHEAPER AND ACTIONABLE: one teacher-forced pass, no decoding and no
grading. On GSM8K, the generation-heaviest benchmark, a single greedy decode of the
same prompts costs a measured 62.6x that pass (501x with self-consistency). The
diagnosis also closes into an act-and-verify loop: PRISM attributes Q6_K SQuAD
damage to the head axis; keeping lm_head in FP16 removes it (bound
76.95 -> 3.81, 20x), and controlled single-axis interventions confirm each term
responds only to its own axis (>= 2.6e5x selectivity, causal, bound holds 26/26).

(iii) STRICTLY MORE THAN CKA, AT NO RANKING COST: our feature arm delta_N IS the
Procrustes size-and-shape distance (App. A.1), and CKA/SVCCA are
rotation-invariant readings of that same geometric agreement, so on identical
features they are a statistical dead heat (our arm -0.002 vs 1-CKA, -0.000 vs
1-SVCCA, bootstrap CIs covering 0; we claim no ranking advantage). Splitting that
residual and lifting it to a bound then costs nothing: the paper's Table 3 ladder
runs Omega_N +0.806 -> delta_N +0.873 -> B_N +0.912. The bound therefore carries
three things none of the similarity scores provides, for free: a risk
certificate, an axis attribution, and a differentiable regularizer.

On what "no grading" means, since it carries the cost argument: a benchmark run
must generate an output per prompt, parse it (GSM8K's "#### <number>", an option
letter, a span), match and normalize it against the gold answer, and aggregate
an accuracy. PRISM does none of these: **its quantity is the CE GAP between two
models over the same gold span (Sec. 5.1), in which correctness plays no part**,
so nothing is generated, parsed, matched or scored. Itemized in full in our
response to Reviewer G3T9 (W1).

A + W-1 + W-6a: usefulness of the bound, and empirical vs population risk

[原文 A]: Clarify usefulness of the bound: The authors should explicitly state whether the
   result should be understood as an empirical/calibration-set bound or a population-risk
   statement. They should also quantify the looseness of the bound and explain whether it
   can support any operational threshold beyond ranking.
[原文 W-1]: Loose risk bound: Reviewers pCi8, G3T9, and 8VrD all note that the proposed
   bound appears useful mainly as a ranking signal, not as an operationally meaningful
   certificate of degradation. This weakens the framing of PRISM as a risk diagnostic
   rather than a calibrated heuristic for ordering variants.
[原文 W-6a]: Clarity and theoretical-framing issues: ... whether the stated risk guarantee
   should be framed more carefully as applying to empirical risk on calibration samples
   rather than population risk. (The presentation half of W-6 is answered after Condition E.)

> **Condition A (with Weak Points 1 and 6a).** Is the bound an
> empirical/calibration-set statement or a population-risk one, how loose is it, and
> can it support any operational threshold beyond ranking?

Response:
We have done all three.

(1) Restatement. Theorem 1 is restated as a bound on the EMPIRICAL risk gap
over the reference sample, the quantity every experiment already reports, and
we add a concentration corollary: under a bounded per-sample loss gap
(`b <= K_feat*D_Z + sqrt(2)*C_z*||W H_T - H_P||_op`, all three factors quantities
PRISM already measures), McDiarmid's inequality gives the population version at
an additive `b*sqrt(2 ln(2/delta)/N)` cost. The constant b is finite by
construction: hidden states are RMSNorm-bounded, so the feature diameter D_Z is
finite and no unbounded-CE pathology arises. The restated theorem and proof are
ready; we can post them verbatim in this thread. No experimental conclusion
changes, since all reported quantities were already empirical.

(2) Looseness, quantified. Across all 570 (family, benchmark, variant) cells
over the paper's five benchmarks: slack B/|dR| has median 1597x, IQR [673,
3966], and is NOT constant (log10 sd ~0.76); this is precisely why the raw
bound is claimed for ranking only. Decomposing the slack: the Lipschitz-constant step
contributes only 0.13-0.40 dex; the remainder comes from the alignment/triangle
steps. Median slack SHRINKS toward low bit-widths (Q8_0: 3.65 dex -> Q2_K: 2.78
dex), i.e., the bound is relatively tightest where degradation is real.

(3) Operational threshold. **Per-(family, benchmark) leave-one-out isotonic
calibration B -> |dR| gives aggregate Leave-one-out MAE 0.055 nats, versus 0.082 for a
predict-the-mean baseline (-33%)**. Leave-one-out means the map is fitted without
the variant it is then asked to predict, so 0.055 is the error on a variant the
calibration has never seen, which is the deployment case; in units of each cell's
own risk range it is a median 15%; the rule "calibrated prediction < 0.1 nats"
reaches precision >= 0.8 in 49/55 cells (21 at 1.0), and >= 0.8 in all 55 at
epsilon = 0.5. (We lead with MAE, which is base-rate-free; the six weaker
precision cells are all multiple-choice benchmarks, ARC/MMLU, where the |dR|
dynamic range is compressed.) The calibration is one-time per family and
transfers across bit-widths; thereafter PRISM answers "is Q4 within epsilon of
the base?" for every new variant with quantified uncertainty and without
running the benchmark on it. We do not claim a benchmark is never needed; we
claim it is needed once per family to fit the calibration, not once per
variant, and even uncalibrated the ranking is already useful.
Uncalibrated B is explicitly scoped to ranking.

B + W-4a: benchmark-independent use and reference-set sensitivity

[原文 B]: Demonstrate benchmark-independent use: The authors should show that PRISM remains
   predictive when computed on a small, benchmark-independent reference set, including
   sensitivity to reference-set size and domain.
[原文 W-4]: Narrow experimental setting: Reviewers G3T9 and 8VrD emphasize that PRISM is
   evaluated largely under teacher-forced feature extraction and benchmark-aligned
   reference data. It remains unclear how well the method transfers to benchmark-
   independent calibration sets, free-running generation, reasoning-heavy tasks, or
   variants that drift farther from the base model.
   (Quoted in full here; its free-running half is answered under Condition D, and its
   last two items at the end of this response.)

> **Condition B (with Weak Point 4).** Show PRISM stays predictive on a small
> benchmark-independent reference set, with size and domain sensitivity. The current
> teacher-forced, benchmark-aligned setting leaves transfer to independent
> calibration sets, free-running generation, reasoning-heavy tasks and
> farther-drifting variants unclear.

Response:
PRISM does not need, and does not claim, a benchmark-independent reference: the
bound is input-conditioned by construction, so diagnosing a task means reading
a small slice of that task's own validation data, which any evaluation already
has, once, with no decoding and no grading. We therefore answer this condition in two parts, as the
meta-review invites: the out-of-scope half (unpaired use) with an explicit
narrowing of the claim, and the in-scope half (how small and stable the
reference can be) with a measurement.

(1) Scope, stated precisely. **The bound is input-conditioned: computed on a
reference set, it bounds the risk gap on that same data**, with the feature and
the risk PAIRED on one distribution (a per-instance guarantee, proved in
App. A; it holds for feature rows of any origin, App. D). Computing the bound
on generic text to predict a different benchmark's degradation would UNPAIR
feature from risk, a distribution-transfer question the derivation does not
cover, so we do not claim it; the revision states this scope in the
Limitations.

(2) The practical requirement behind the condition is minimal, and we state it
exactly. The reference is not a curated calibration corpus; it is a handful of
the diagnosed task's own validation items, each read as its prompt followed by
its reference answer (features and per-token CE come from that answer region,
Sec. 5.1). What PRISM removes is therefore the decoding and the grading, not
the validation data: it reads the slice once, whereas a benchmark run must
generate an output per prompt, parse it, match it against the gold answer, and
score an accuracy. Because PRISM's quantity is the CE GAP between two models on
identical tokens, in which correctness plays no part, no parsing, matching or
scoring is needed at any point; and in this default protocol nothing is
generated either.

(3) The in-scope measurement: sensitivity to reference-set size.
Two takeaways. (i) The ranking is
SIZE-INVARIANT: rs against each benchmark's |dR| is identical (to four decimals)
across sizes {8, 16, 32, 64, 128} and 3 draws per size, so even 8 sequences
reproduce the full ranking and 32 is not a lucky draw. (ii) The across-draw
NOISE IS SMALL AND SHRINKS WITH SIZE: the mean coefficient of variation falls
from 3.7% (n=8) to 1.2% (n=128). The ranking holds even where adjacent bounds
are close because variants sharing a reference set shift together (common-mode),
so the per-draw order is preserved in all 15 settings. Bound_I, mean +- sd over
3 draws (Llama):

Domain enters only on the regularizer side, where a generic reference is
in-protocol. Regularizer-side sensitivity (trace, lambda = 0.5, a point in the
paper's swept range; Llama-TruthfulQA, lr 1e-5; measured in the extended

W-4's two remaining items, for completeness. Reasoning-heavy tasks: answered

   simpler feature-preserving scores are needed. For regularization, stronger
   continual-learning baselines such as EWC, SLoRA, CLAIM, ArMA, or layer-freezing/replay
   variants would substantially improve the evaluation.
[原文 W-3]: Incomplete empirical comparisons: The paper positions PRISM against
   representation similarity metrics and catastrophic-forgetting methods, but reviewers
   G3T9, pCi8, and 8VrD argue that the evaluation lacks sufficiently strong or direct
   baselines in both the diagnostic/ranking setting and the regularization setting.
[原文 W-5]: Unconvincing regularization results: Reviewers eQL6, pCi8, and 8VrD find the
   shape regularizer promising but not yet fully validated. The evaluation uses limited
   baselines, shows mixed results across settings, and does not clearly establish the
   tradeoff between reducing forgetting and preserving new-task performance.
   (Quoted in full here; its "mixed results" half is answered under Condition E.)

> **Condition C (with Weak Points 3 and 5).** Add stronger baselines on both sides:
> CKA, SVCCA and simpler feature-preserving scores for the diagnostic, and stronger
> continual-learning baselines (EWC, SLoRA, CLAIM, ArMA, layer-freezing/replay) for
> the regularizer.

Response:
Both baseline families now exist under the paper's exact protocol, and the
headline is deliberately honest: **on ranking, the bound's extra structure costs
NOTHING, so a certified risk bound, an axis attribution and a differentiable
penalty come for free relative to a similarity score**; and on regularization the
shape penalty leads EWC, L2-SP, layer-freezing, and replay at matched target-task
loss on TruthfulQA so far.
Diagnostics: we re-extract features for every variant of both main-text
families under the identical protocol (same proxies, same 512-sample
token-level features) and compute linear-CKA, SVCCA, and Procrustes distance
on the SAME features, |dR| joined from the paper's tables (n=12/11 Llama/Qwen,
the full variant sets). We report two blocks and keep their provenance separate,
because mixing feature extractions would make the comparison unreadable.

Block 1, the same-features comparison the condition asks for. All three rows are
computed on the IDENTICAL re-extracted features, so this is apples-to-apples;
mean Spearman rs(score, |dR|) over the five benchmarks:

| score (identical re-extracted features) | Llama | Qwen3 | mean r_s |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| 1-CKA | +0.924 | +0.882 | +0.903 |
| 1-SVCCA | +0.924 | +0.878 | +0.901 |
| our feature arm delta_N (= the Procrustes distance) | +0.930 | +0.873 | +0.901 |

Block 2, what the bound's components contribute, taken from the paper's own
Table 3 (a single pipeline, W_N gauge, cumulative rows):

| component (paper Table 3, W_N block) | Llama | Qwen3 | mean r_s |
|:--|--:|--:|--:|
| Omega_N (shape core alone) | +0.839 | +0.773 | +0.806 |
| delta_N (+ scale; = the Procrustes distance) | +0.898 | +0.847 | +0.873 |
| B_N (+ head; full certified bound) | +0.927 | +0.896 | +0.912 |

[Provenance, so the two blocks are read correctly. Block 1 recomputes every score
on ONE fresh extraction, which is what makes the CKA/SVCCA comparison
like-for-like; Block 2 quotes the paper's own round. Absolute levels therefore
differ slightly between blocks, and every claim below uses only WITHIN-block
comparisons.]

The dead heat does not rest on any single cell, and we checked this three ways.
Because all three scores are read from the SAME features they move together, so
what matters is the difference, not the level: (i) the largest gap between our
feature arm and either similarity score in any of the ten (family, benchmark)
cells is 0.036; (ii) dropping any one of the five benchmarks changes the pooled
paired difference by at most 0.006; and (iii) two independent extractions that
differ 3.2x in token budget reproduce every cell of the comparison to four
decimals. No single benchmark and no extraction detail drives the result.

Why Block 1 lands together, stated precisely. **Our feature arm delta_N IS the
Procrustes size-and-shape distance: delta_N^2 = (rho_T - rho_P)^2 +
2 rho_T rho_P (1 - Omega_N) = (||Z_T||_F^2 + ||Z_P||_F^2 - 2||Z_T^T Z_P||_*)/n,
which is exactly the squared orthogonal-Procrustes residual (App. A.1). So
Procrustes is not an external competitor but the feature half of our own bound,
and CKA/SVCCA are rotation-invariant readings of that same geometric agreement**.
On identical features a paired bootstrap over variants (5000 resamples) puts our
feature arm at -0.002 versus 1-CKA (95% CI [-0.038, +0.011]) and -0.000 versus
1-SVCCA ([-0.014, +0.019]): statistically indistinguishable, which is the
expected outcome once the algebra above is granted.
We are careful not to overstate the relation. CKA is NOT substitutable into our
residual: it differs in its denominator, is related to our shape core only by the
inequality CKA >= Omega_F^2, and is not derived from any alignment optimization
(App. B). It is a close relative, not the same object. Symmetrically, our
contribution was never the Procrustes distance itself; as App. A.1 states, it is
(a) the explicit SPLIT of that residual into a scale term and a shape term, which
the shape-metric literature treats as one descriptive dissimilarity and which
Sec. 4 shows dominate under different lifecycle settings, and (b) its LIFTING to
a functional-risk bound (Theorem 1).

What we do and do not claim, in two within-block steps. (i) We do NOT claim a
ranking advantage over CKA/SVCCA: on identical features Block 1 is a statistical
dead heat, and we report it as such. (ii) Within the paper's own round, adding the
remaining bound components does not cost ranking but adds to it: +0.067 from the
scale arm (Omega_N -> delta_N) and +0.039 from the head arm (delta_N -> B_N).
Chaining the two: our feature arm sits at the CKA/SVCCA level, and the full
certified bound sits at or above its own feature arm. So the certified bound, the
axis attribution and the differentiable penalty are obtained at no ranking cost
relative to a similarity score.
Those three are what a similarity score cannot supply. (a) A RISK BOUND: none of
CKA/SVCCA/Procrustes bounds |dR| at all. (b) AXIS ATTRIBUTION (Sec. 5.3): the
bound separates scale, shape, and head, so it localizes WHICH axis drove a
variant's degradation, not merely that it drifted. The identity gauge B_I, for
instance, leads on extraction-QA (SQuAD +0.811/+0.955), where its scale arm
carries the ranking signal a similarity score cannot see. (c) A DIFFERENTIABLE
REGULARIZER (Sec. 5.4). Since CKA is bounded only by the inequality
CKA >= Omega_F^2 and is not alignment-derived (App. B), it cannot define the
attribution, let alone the bound or the training signal.
Regularization: the shape regularizer is a demonstration that the framework's diagnosed
axis is actionable, so its matched baselines are general forgetting methods in our
single-task setting. As the meta-review suggests, we add layer-freezing (LoRA on the
top-K layers, K in {4,8,16}) alongside EWC (diagonal Fisher) and L2-SP, all under the
paper's exact protocol, each reported at the config whose target-task loss matches the
shape run's (equal plasticity, so a baseline is not rewarded for under-training). On

all three seeds are

[Summary]
PRISM is a diagnostic tool for post-training LLM variants — quantized, LoRA fine-tuned, or
distilled. The core problem it solves is pretty simple: when you quantize a model, you want
to know how much you broke it and where, without running a full benchmark suite. Existing
similarity metrics like CKA and SVCCA can tell you that a variant has drifted from the base,
but not why or which component is responsible. PRISM derives a closed-form upper bound on
the cross-entropy risk gap between a base model and its variant, decomposed into three
independently measurable axes: scale mismatch (activation magnitude collapse), shape
mismatch (distortion of the relative geometry between token representations), and head
divergence (the prediction head itself drifting). The key technical insight is that the
linear lm_head plus near-isometric backbone structure of modern LLMs lets you express this
bound tightly — avoiding the naive Lipschitz constant that would blow up with vocabulary
size — and the Procrustes residual gives you an exact scale/shape decomposition for free.
Empirically it ranks PTQ variants with Spearman rs = 0.820 and LoRA forgetting checkpoints
at rs = 0.831 across two model families and five benchmarks, using only a single forward
pass per model with no labels. The shape term is also differentiable, so it doubles as a
regularizer during LoRA fine-tuning that outperforms experience replay at mitigating
catastrophic forgetting.

[Strengths]
- Exact decomposition. The scale/shape split is an exact identity, not an approximation.
  The math is clean and the derivation is self-contained.
- Tighter Lipschitz constant. The simplex polarization trick is a real contribution — the
  naive spectral bound scales with vocabulary size and becomes useless at V~10⁵, this one
  doesn't.
- Three axes are actually distinct. Empirically they do capture different failure modes
  rather than just being correlated proxies of the same thing.
- Low compute cost. One forward pass per model, no labels needed. Useful in practice when
  you're choosing between a dozen quantization variants and don't want to run full
  benchmarks on all of them.
- Shape regularizer is a natural byproduct. You get a differentiable training objective for
  free from the bound, and it actually outperforms replay in the experiments.

2) W2 [原文]: The bound is loose by orders of magnitude against actual risk gap, and the

We agree the bound is loose, and instead of defending the number we can say where
the looseness comes from. Slack B/|dR| has median 1597x over 570 cells, and it
decomposes into identifiable worst-case steps (numbers for Llama-3.1-8B):

* **K_feat is a worst case twice over.** It is the largest pairwise distance
  between lm_head columns, 2.6137 here. The largest per-token sensitivity actually
  observed is 1.645 (1.6x smaller), and the MEAN observed sensitivity is 0.413 (a
  further 4.0x), so K_feat sits 6.3x, or 0.80 dex, above the sensitivity the risk
  gap actually experiences.
* **The shape term carries a large scale prefactor.** delta^2 contains
  2 rho_T rho_P (1 - Omega), and 2 rho_T rho_P is about 3.9e4 on Llama/MMLU, so a
  dimensionless residual of 1 - Omega = 0.024 for Q4_K_M becomes a shape term of

  feature norm times head norm, but it means B is measured in nats times a large

* **The inequality chain sums two legs instead of letting them cancel.** Target to
  hybrid and hybrid to proxy are bounded separately and added, then averaged over

  tier, the K step accounts for 0.13-0.40 dex of the slack while this remainder

**Two of those three are constant within a cell, and that is precisely why the paper
reports an ordering and computes rank correlations rather than absolute risk.**
K_feat and K_pred depend only on the target head, so they are identical for every

INFLATE every variant's bound by essentially the same multiple: they move the value

computes its rank correlations, which is why the design and the looseness are two
sides of one fact rather than a coincidence. What can still move the order is only
the third source, the alignment and triangle remainder, which does vary per
variant; that is both why the rank correlations are near 0.9 rather than 1.0 and
why no fixed multiplicative correction exists (log10 sd of the slack ~0.76). The
ranking claim is therefore not a retreat from an absolute one; it is the claim the
construction supports.

6) W6 [原文]: The whole orthogonal alignment setup relies on representations being
   near-isometric across related models. The paper cites two papers to justify this but
   doesn't discuss whether the assumption still holds after aggressive quantization, when
   feature geometry is already severely distorted.

> **W6.** The orthogonal-alignment setup relies on near-isometric representations.
> Two citations are given, but the paper never discusses whether that still holds
> after aggressive quantization, when feature geometry is already severely
> distorted.

Response:
The assumption affects only how LOOSE the bound is, never whether it holds.
**Theorem 1 is a family of bounds indexed by W, and the only condition is that W be
orthogonal: every W in O(d) gives a valid inequality (Thm. 1, L132; proof App.
C.1).** The geometry is why: an orthogonal W is a rotation or a reflection and
nothing more, so it re-orients the proxy's feature cloud against the target's
without changing a single length, distance or angle inside it. **Our gauge is
therefore an exact isometry BY CONSTRUCTION; what is empirical is only how well such
a map can be made to match the two clouds.** The choice of W changes how the
mismatch is split between the feature arm (delta) and the head arm (gamma), never
the geometry being split.

The gauge is therefore, in the paper's own words, "primarily design-driven"
(L145-152), and our two sit at opposite ends of that split. W = I is the identity:
compare the features in their own coordinates, keep gamma at its minimum, and stay
differentiable, which is what the Sec. 3.5 regularizer needs. W_N = V U^T from the
SVD Z_T^T Z_P = U S V^T is the orthogonal-Procrustes solution, the best rigid
re-orientation: it minimizes delta instead, paying a per-step SVD and a nonzero
gamma. A W optimized under any other objective over O(d) is equally admissible and
moves only the tightness. Near-isometry is not a precondition, only one more entry
in the looseness itemized under W2.

What survives is therefore a quantitative question, and it is the reviewer's: how
much looseness does the orthogonality restriction cost once the geometry really is
badly distorted? We measure it at the bit-widths named. We align the features twice
in their top-r principal subspace, once with the best rotation-and-scale and once
with the best arbitrary linear map, which stretches and shears and so serves only as
an oracle ceiling, and report iso_gain = 1 - res_linear / res_orthogonal, the
fraction of residual the restriction costs, 0 meaning free. Means over MMLU and
SQuAD on Llama-3.1-8B, excluding noise-floor rows: **the FP16 dtype control sits at
0.084, this metric's own noise level, and quantization reaches only 0.130 (Q8_0),
0.165 (Q4_K_M), 0.183 (Q2_K) and 0.189 (NF4), the worst single cell reaching 0.29.
Even at 2 bits, dropping orthogonality entirely would shrink the residual by under a
fifth on average.**

The same measurement answers "already severely distorted" from the other side: even
after the oracle linear alignment, 26% (Q4_K_M) and 57% (Q2_K) of the relative
residual remains on MMLU. Most of what aggressive quantization does is not linearly
alignable at all, so the bound is charging genuine degradation, not an artefact of
the orthogonal restriction.

**In sum:** the three answers share one shape. We locate the looseness instead of
disputing it, and two of its three sources are cell constants, which is why the
paper reports an ordering (W2). On identical features the bound cedes nothing to
CKA or SVCCA while carrying a risk certificate, an axis attribution and a
trainable objective that they cannot (W3). And the near-isometry assumption is
measured rather than assumed: it affects only tightness, by under a fifth of the
residual even at 2 bits (W6). We hope these speak to the practical-significance
concern, and we are glad to engage further on any point that remains.

[Summary]
This paper proposes PRISM, a framework for diagnosing performance degradation for
post-trained LLM models. The key idea is to compare the hidden representations and output
heads of a base model and its finetuned model, and to decompose the resulting cross-entropy
risk gap into three interpretable components: scale mismatch, shape mismatch, and head
divergence. The authors derive a closed-form upper bound on the risk difference and show
that the resulting PRISM score correlates with empirical degradation across quantization
and LoRA fine-tuning settings. Beyond post-hoc diagnosis, the paper further uses the
differentiable shape component as a regularizer during frozen-head LoRA training, aiming to
mitigate catastrophic forgetting without relying on experience replay.

[Strengths]
- The paper addresses how to how degradation from post-training can be explained in theory,
  rather than merely detecting that their performance has dropped. The proposed
  decomposition into scale, shape, and head components is intuitive and provides a more
  diagnostic view than single-number representation similarity metrics such as CKA or SVCCA.
- A key strength is the connection between representation geometry and cross-entropy risk.
  It gives a principled justification for using geometric drift as a proxy for model
  degradation. The decomposition also helps localize different failure modes, such as
  representation distortion in low-bit quantization or head divergence in GGUF-style
  quantization.
- The empirical evaluation is reasonably broad, covering multiple model families,
  quantization backends, bit-widths, and LoRA forgetting scenarios. The rank correlations
  reported for both PTQ and LoRA settings suggest that PRISM is useful as a model-ranking
  and diagnostic tool.

Response:
The load-bearing reason is the linear head, not the representation hypotheses. Our
own presentation hides that: L112-115 motivates the restriction by empirical
isometry, **which says why orthogonality is CHEAP, not why it is NECESSARY.** The
necessity came from a workflow we needed before we had any theory. We had to compare
many quantized and fine-tuned variants of the same base model, filter the clearly
decayed ones without running a full benchmark suite on each, and be told WHICH axis
to repair. **That asks two things of the quantity: the damage must not be fittable
away, and whatever remains must divide into parts one can act on.** Steps (1) and
(2) are those two requirements; (3) is where the hypotheses come in.

(1) Why nothing larger than O(d) is admissible. Because the head is linear, a
model's features and head are only defined jointly, up to an invertible map: Z_P H_P
and (Z_P A)(A^-1 H_P) are the same proxy model. **So a feature discrepancy measured
after an unrestricted invertible alignment is not identifiable: the damage can
always be pushed through A into the head and fitted away.** O(d) is exactly the set
of linear maps that leaves the geometry the head reads out untouched: an orthogonal
W only RE-ORIENTS the proxy's feature cloud, preserving every norm, distance and
angle inside it, so what it leaves is distortion the head genuinely has to absorb.

(2) What the restriction buys: two axes that can be read separately. Over O(d) the
mean squared feature residual splits with no approximation into `(rho_T - rho_P)^2`
plus `2 rho_T rho_P (1 - Omega_W)` (Prop. 1, L125), and the rho are MEASURED RMS
feature scales rather than fitted parameters, which is what lets scale mismatch and
shape mismatch be attributed separately. Under a general linear map the identity is
unavailable, since the map itself carries scale and shear; larger groups do have
closed-form solutions, just not a residual that still means anything.

(3) Where the hypotheses enter, and all they are needed for: they make the
restriction cheap. The linear/platonic results say that related models tend to align
well under near-orthogonal maps, which is why a single orthogonal W is SUFFICIENT in
practice, i.e. why the bound is tight rather than vacuous. Quantitatively: on
Llama-3.1-8B, lifting the restriction to an unrestricted linear map shrinks the
feature residual by under a fifth on average even at 2 bits, and by at most 0.29 in
any single cell. **Validity does not depend on any of this: Theorem 1 holds for ANY
W in O(d) (L132, App. C.1), so if the hypotheses were false the bound would merely
loosen, never break.**

One distinction is worth making explicit, since it is the likeliest residue of the
confusion: the paper chooses at two levels. The GROUP is forced, by (1) and (2).
Which MEMBER of it we use is not: we report W = I and the Procrustes W_N, and the
paper calls that choice "primarily design-driven" (L145-152), because both are valid
and differ only in tightness and in which term carries the mismatch.
