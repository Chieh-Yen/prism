Title 欄:Closing Note for the Area Chair: Scope Clarification

As the discussion window closes, we record one clarification. The evidence for the five requests in the meta-review is already indexed in the Point A to Point E comments and the summary comment of 30 July, and in the per-reviewer summaries of 2 August, so we do not repeat it here.

Reviewer eQL6's counterexample showed that PRISM's attribution is coordinate-relative: the paired map $(Z_P,H_P)\mapsto(Z_PA,A^{-1}H_P)$, applied before the diagnosis, leaves the model's outputs unchanged while redistributing the scale, shape, and head terms, so functionally equivalent parameterizations can receive different diagnoses. Modifying both the backbone and the head does not by itself trigger this. Our evaluated variants are stored in the inherited coordinates of a shared checkpoint, and the reviewer's own assessment was that this is "likely not an issue in the post-training variants studied in the paper". No reported value changes, Theorem 1 remains valid for each specified $W\in O(d)$, and no component is required to remain frozen. For jointly optimized backbone-head checkpoints the shared convention is not guaranteed, and comparable attribution there would require a canonicalization criterion PRISM does not provide. We agree this belongs in the main text: the revision states the convention, adds the exact $O(d)$-equivariance remark, places attribution after joint backbone-head optimization outside the validated scope, replaces the earlier "identifiable from the bound's decomposition" wording, and leaves canonicalization for full SFT and RLHF as future work.

The result is a paper whose scope is stated more precisely than at submission, in the direction the meta-review invited: the guarantee is stated on the empirical calibration-set gap, with a concentration corollary to the population form; the diagnostic scope is stated as input-conditioned; GSM8K is kept as a stated limitation; and the coordinate convention is now explicit. On GSM8K the boundary is gap magnitude rather than task type: the mean gap under quantization is about 0.019 nats, an order of magnitude below the other benchmarks, while on the same task under LoRA, where the gap is 0.134, the ordering holds; a mitigation we tested is reported as failing. Each of these rests on the experiments or the analysis posted in this discussion rather than on wording alone. Should anything remain open for the Area Chair or for any reviewer, we are glad to address it in the time that remains. We thank Reviewer eQL6 for pressing this boundary to its precise form, and the Area Chair and reviewers for a discussion that strengthened both the evidence and the statement of scope.
