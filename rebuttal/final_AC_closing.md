Title 欄:Closing Note for the Area Chair: Scope Clarification

As the discussion window closes, we record one clarification. The evidence for the five requests in the meta-review is already indexed in the Point A to Point E comments and the summary comment of 30 July, and in the per-reviewer summaries of 2 August, so we do not repeat it here.

Reviewer eQL6's counterexample showed that PRISM's axis attribution is not invariant over the full $GL(d)$ equivalence class: functionally equivalent parameterizations can receive different scale, shape, and head diagnoses. We agree, and we will make this explicit in the main text. Its reach is on how the attribution is interpreted rather than on Theorem 1 or on the values we report: the bound remains valid for each stored model and specified $W\in O(d)$, and no component is required to remain frozen. Our evaluated comparisons are between variants derived from a shared checkpoint in its inherited parameterization, so no compensating change of basis is introduced; the reviewer's own assessment was that this is "likely not an issue in the post-training variants studied in the paper", while flagging variants that optimize both the backbone and the head as the case where that convention is not guaranteed. We share that reading: comparing attributions across jointly optimized checkpoints would require an additional canonicalization criterion. The revision therefore states the convention explicitly, adds the exact $O(d)$-equivariance boundary, places attribution after joint backbone-head optimization outside the validated scope, and replaces the earlier "identifiable from the bound's decomposition" wording.

The result is a paper whose scope is stated more precisely than at submission, in the direction the meta-review invited: the guarantee is stated on the empirical calibration-set gap, with a concentration corollary to the population form; the diagnostic scope is stated as input-conditioned; GSM8K is kept as a stated limitation; and the coordinate convention is now explicit. On GSM8K the boundary is gap magnitude rather than task type: the mean gap under quantization is about 0.019 nats, an order of magnitude below the other benchmarks, while on the same task under LoRA, where the gap is 0.134, the ordering holds; a mitigation we tested is reported as failing. Each of these rests on the experiments or the analysis posted in this discussion rather than on wording alone. We thank Reviewer eQL6 for pressing this boundary to its precise form, and the Area Chair and reviewers for a discussion that strengthened both the evidence and the statement of scope.
