Title 欄:Closing Note for the Area Chair: Scope Clarification

As the discussion window closes, we record one clarification. The evidence addressing the five requests in the meta-review is already indexed in Points A–E, the 30 July summary, and the 2 August per-reviewer summaries, so we do not repeat it here.

Reviewer eQL6's follow-up clarified that PRISM's attribution is coordinate-relative under the paired map $(Z_P,H_P)\mapsto(Z_PA,A^{-1}H_P)$, which leaves the model's outputs unchanged while redistributing the scale, shape, and head terms. The ambiguity requires this paired transformation; changing both the backbone and head is not sufficient. None of the evaluated procedures applies this transformation: all variants are compared in their inherited coordinates. Consistent with this, Reviewer eQL6 noted that the issue is "likely not an issue in the post-training variants studied in the paper". Accordingly, this clarification changes no reported numerical value; Theorem 1 remains valid for every specified $W\in O(d)$, and no component must remain frozen. We agree that this interpretive boundary belongs in the main text: the revision states the coordinate convention, adds the exact $O(d)$-equivariance remark, places attribution after joint backbone-head optimization outside the validated scope, replaces the earlier "identifiable from the bound's decomposition" wording, and leaves canonicalization for coordinate-comparable attribution in that regime as future work.

The result is a paper whose scope is stated more precisely than at submission, in the direction the meta-review invited: the guarantee is stated on the empirical calibration-set gap, with a concentration corollary to the population form; the diagnostic scope is stated as input-conditioned; GSM8K is kept as a stated limitation; and the coordinate convention is now explicit. On GSM8K, the evidence points to gap magnitude rather than task identity: the mean gap under quantization is about 0.019 nats, substantially below the other benchmarks, while on the same task under LoRA, where the gap is 0.134, the ordering holds; a mitigation we tested is reported as failing. Each of these rests on the experiments or the analysis posted in this discussion rather than on wording alone. Should anything remain open for the Area Chair or for any reviewer, we are glad to address it in the time that remains. We thank Reviewer eQL6 for pressing this boundary to its precise form, and the Area Chair and reviewers for a discussion that strengthened both the evidence and the statement of scope.
