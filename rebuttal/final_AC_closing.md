Title 欄:Closing Note for the Area Chair: What the Final Exchanges Settled

As the discussion window closes, we record what the final exchanges settled. Every point raised after the summaries has been answered in the relevant thread, and the evidence addressing the five requests in the meta-review is already indexed in Points A–E, the 30 July summary, and the 2 August per-reviewer summaries, so we do not repeat it here.

**The coordinate convention.** Reviewer eQL6's counterexample showed that PRISM's attribution is coordinate-relative: the paired map $(Z_P,H_P)\mapsto(Z_PA,A^{-1}H_P)$, applied before the diagnosis, leaves the model's outputs unchanged while redistributing the scale, shape, and head terms. The ambiguity requires that the two changes be exact inverses of each other; modifying both the backbone and the head is not sufficient, and no evaluated procedure applies such a pair: all variants are compared in their inherited coordinates. Both reviewers who examined this agree on its reach. Reviewer eQL6 noted the issue is "likely not an issue in the post-training variants studied in the paper", and Reviewer 8VrD judged the convention "reasonable for the PTQ and frozen-head LoRA settings studied here" and not a defect in the bound. Both regard the root-cause interpretation as unsettled where backbone and head are jointly updated, and that is where we now make no claim. No reported value changes, Theorem 1 remains valid for every specified $W\in O(d)$, and no component must remain frozen. The revision states the convention in the main text, adds the exact $O(d)$-equivariance remark, places attribution after joint backbone-head optimization outside the validated scope, replaces the earlier "identifiable" wording, and leaves canonicalization for that regime as future work.

**What the final exchange added.** Reviewer 8VrD identified the sharpest remaining comparison: the paper does not separate the bound from a simpler likelihood screen. The measured cross-entropy gap comes from the same teacher-forced pass, so there is no cost advantage to claim against such a screen; the revision will report that gap beside the bound, and will not claim a better ordering without the comparison. What the same pass does add is the decomposition: which axis moved, and a differentiable penalty on the quantity diagnosed. We also accept the scope described there: task-relevant reference data, within-setting calibration, closely related variants of a shared checkpoint, and a workflow claim confined to the settings in which the interventions and the regularizer were validated.

**Where this leaves the paper.** The scope is stated more precisely than at submission, in the direction the meta-review invited: the guarantee is stated on the empirical calibration-set gap, with a concentration corollary to the population form; the diagnostic scope is input-conditioned; and GSM8K is kept as a stated limitation. On GSM8K the evidence points to gap magnitude rather than task identity: the mean gap under quantization is about 0.019 nats, substantially below the other benchmarks, while on the same task under LoRA, where the gap is 0.134, the ordering holds; a mitigation we tested is reported as failing. Each of these rests on the experiments or the analysis posted in this discussion rather than on wording alone.

Should anything remain open for the Area Chair or for any reviewer, we are glad to address it in the time that remains. We thank the reviewers and the Area Chair for a discussion that sharpened both the evidence and the statement of scope.
